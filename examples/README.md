# Examples

Thirteen examples, each going on from the one before: a roaming network whose operator three organizations hold, a
charging station operator asking to be added to it with the manufacturers of its stations and meters, its pools, their
templates and their groups, checkpoints of the history, with a correction between two of them, witnesses signing them,
the history compacted after them with an archive, price statements named by checkpoints, witnesses checking them, what a
driver's app keeps as evidence of the price that applied, a new epoch of post-quantum signatures, the status of an
EVSE pushed live over the WebSocket of the history, and who checks the meter: a transparency software and its
certificate, each signed by who states it. They are C# with POI 3 alone, without a hub, in one console project,
[csharp/](csharp/); A2 and A3 also show the same with the
[RoamingHubCLI](https://github.com/OpenChargingCloud/RoamingHubCLI): at its prompt or over SSH, in its web interface and
over HTTP.

| | What it does | Revisions | Signed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Example A1](exampleA1.md) | **The creation of the roaming network `QA`, and how to check it.** The roaming network operator `OCC`, whose keys three organizations hold, two of them deciding via a 2/3 quorum, states its identity keys and quorum in a key statement, each organization certifies its own signing key, `QA` is made, and its trust anchor published as a QR code. Then `QA` is checked from the QR code and what is published alone. | 0 | `OCC`, its three holders |
| [Example A2](exampleA2.md) | **The charging station operator `DE*GEF`.** The operator makes two identity keys and a signing key for each usage, keeps them in `DE-GEF/`, makes the change set adding it and the manufacturers of its charging stations and energy meters, `GraphDefined`, `SAALE` and `KERNBERG`, parties with keys of their own, and each signs it with its identity keys, as the proof that it holds them; two holders of `OCC` sign it as well. Then the manufacturers state their models, `GD-DC300`, `GD-AC44`, `KDM-500` and `SM-3`, each with its `models` key; signed by `OCC`, they are refused. With the hub: it signs as the RNO and lets the change set of each party wait for its signatures, the manufacturers on their own. | 1, 2 | the change set: the identity keys of `DE*GEF` and its manufacturers and two holders of `OCC`; the models: their manufacturers; the commits: `OCC` |
| [Example A3](exampleA3.md) | **Two pools, their templates and their groups.** `DE*GEF` adds the templates of a DC and an AC station, their EVSEs and the energy meters of these, two pools of a DC and an AC station of two EVSEs each, every EVSE with its energy meter, and a station group and an EVSE group for each current; then the pools as they are at runtime. Only its own program can do it: nobody else has a key of `DE*GEF`; signed by its key for its tariffs, or by `OCC`, a pool is refused. | 3 to 7 | `DE*GEF`, its `infrastructure` key |
| [Example A4](exampleA4.md) | **The first checkpoint, and joining from it.** `OCC` makes the first checkpoint of `QA`, of revision 7, a signed statement over the Merkle root of its commits; `DE*GEF` adds two pools more, their AC stations recorded with 44 kW and EVSEs of 22 kW; then `QA` is joined from the checkpoint's snapshot and the commits since, a commit is proved to be part of the checkpoint, and changed checkpoints, snapshots and proofs are refused. | checkpoint 0; 8, 9 | the checkpoint: `OCC`, its three holders; the commits: `DE*GEF` |
| [Example A5](exampleA5.md) | **A correction between two checkpoints.** `OCC` makes checkpoint 1, of revision 9, `DE*GEF` corrects the AC stations to 22 kW with EVSEs of 11 kW, `OCC` makes checkpoint 2, of revision 10; then the line of checkpoints is checked, and the AC stations at every checkpoint: commit by commit from checkpoint 0, and from each checkpoint's snapshot on its own. | checkpoints 1, 2; 10 | as in A4 |
| [Example A6](exampleA6.md) | **Witnesses sign the checkpoints.** A calibration authority and an association of operators each take the history of `QA` from its genesis snapshot, commit by commit, check the three checkpoints against it and sign them; `OCC` takes their signatures. A second checkpoint of a sequence, a split view, is refused by a witness; a verifier requires `OCC` and both witnesses, and a proof of a commit is evidence of the history they saw. | checkpoints 0 to 2 | the witnesses, with keys trusted by configuration |
| [Example A7](exampleA7.md) | **Compaction with an archive, after the checkpoints.** `OCC` compacts the history of `QA` to the commit of checkpoint 0, then of checkpoint 1, archiving what it drops, one segment each; dropped commits and their proofs come from the archive, also after a restart. Then each segment alone gives the versions from its root to its end again, its end the one its checkpoint states, its commits the Merkle root the checkpoint signs. | none new | - |
| [Example A8](exampleA8.md) | **Price statements, named by checkpoints.** `DE*GEF` adds an AC and a DC charging tariff, its EVSE templates name them, `OCC` makes checkpoint 3; `DE*GEF` states ad hoc prices of its DC EVSEs, signed price statements chained per EVSE, then raises its AC tariff by a commit, and checkpoint 4 names the statements. They are checked against the keys of `DE*GEF` and the tree of checkpoint 4; a proof shows one was made between the two checkpoints; a split view and a statement signed by the infrastructure key are refused. | 11 to 13; checkpoints 3, 4 | `DE*GEF`, its `tariffs` and `infrastructure` keys |
| [Example A9](exampleA9.md) | **Witnesses check the price statements.** The witnesses of A6 go on with their copies of the history: they take the commits of A8 and the checkpoints 3 and 4, and sign checkpoint 4 only after they checked the price statements it names; without them, with one missing or one changed, they refuse. Then the proof of a price statement is witnessed evidence. | checkpoints 3, 4 | the witnesses |
| [Example A10](exampleA10.md) | **What a driver's app keeps as evidence.** Before charging at `DE*GEF*E1*1`, the app gets the proof of its charging station, attested by the holders of `OCC`, and the price statement of the EVSE, and checks both offline against the trust anchor; after checkpoint 5 it adds the proof of the station's version in checkpoint 4, of the statement in checkpoint 5, and both checkpoints, signed by the witnesses. Months later, when the attestations have ended, the evidence still shows the tariff and the price that applied when charging began; another price, another time, another station and a checkpoint without witnesses are refused. | 14; checkpoint 5 | `DE*GEF`, its `tariffs` key; the witnesses |
| [Example A11](exampleA11.md) | **New locks, same house: epoch 2.** The holders of `OCC`, `DE*GEF` and the witnesses make ML-DSA keys, `OCC` states them next to its old ones, signed by the old quorum, and `DE*GEF` replaces its keys; the final checkpoint 6 closes epoch 1 and names epoch 2, of SHA-512 and ML-DSA only, whose genesis is its version. The first commit of epoch 2 takes the old keys out, and its first checkpoint is signed with the old and the new keys: a verifier trusting only the trust anchor and the witnesses of epoch 1 follows, one trusting only the new ones joins from it. Old keys, a commit of epoch 1 and a genesis of another version are refused; epoch 1 stays readable. | 15, 16; checkpoint 6, final; epoch 2: 0 to 2, checkpoint 0 | the quorum of `OCC`, old and new keys; `DE*GEF`; the witnesses, old and new keys |
| [Example A12](exampleA12.md) | **The atlas and the traffic report, live.** `OCC` serves `QA` of epoch 2 with `POI_API`, a mirror follows it with a `RoamingNetworkPeer`, trusting the trust anchor of epoch 2, and serves its copy; a driver's app subscribes there. `DE*GEF` sets the status, the energy status and the price status of `DE*GEF*E1*1` at the server of `OCC`, with its token, and every change is pushed to the app at once; a late report, the app setting a status and another token are refused, and the head stays the same commit. Then `DE*GEF` lowers its DC tariff over the same WebSocket, and the commit is pushed as well, signed and checked against the trust anchor. Subscriptions end with the connection. | epoch 2: 3 | `DE*GEF`, its ML-DSA `tariffs` key; `OCC` |
| [Example A13](exampleA13.md) | **Who checks the meter.** `OCC` adds the transparency software manufacturer `GraphDefined`, the PTB, notified body `0102` for the `MID`, both with their keys, and the BSI, for the `CRA`, without keys: it does not take part yet. `GraphDefined` states its release of Chargy with the end of its support period, its declaration of conformity and its bill of materials, signed by its `software` key; the PTB certifies the station model `GD-DC300` under the `MID` with the meter model `KDM-500` and Chargy as its components, and the `KDM-500` with Chargy, signed by its `certificates` key; signed by `OCC`, a release is refused, and so is a certificate of the PTB under the `CRA`. `DE*GEF` names the certificates for its DC stations and their energy meters, and Chargy under the German calibration law for the meters; the station proof of `DE*GEF*E1*1` gives the release and both certificates in the skeletons of their manufacturer and body. | epoch 2: 4 to 7 | the quorum of `OCC`; `GraphDefined`; the PTB; `DE*GEF` |

Run the examples in [csharp/](csharp/), where their output stays:

```
dotnet run
```

A run first removes what the last one wrote, then writes the private keys of the organizations, of `DE*GEF`, of
`GraphDefined` and of the PTB into
directories of their own, what is published into `published/`, the keys of the witnesses a verifier trusts into
`trusted/`, what the driver's app keeps into `driver/`, and the history of `QA` into `poi/QA/`, of its epoch 2 into
`poi/QA-epoch-2/`, all ignored by git. What POI 3 is, its entities and its change sets, is in [docs/](../docs/README.md)
and the [README](../README.md) of WWCP_POI.
